PSD Guide

You have an image of coffee particles taken via a microscope with a fixed field of view (so that all images are easily co-analysed). You've measured the width of that field, in mm, and you enter that near the top to provide an absolute scale. There is a trade-off of narrow width and high resolution but fewer particles, and wide width, lower resolution but more particles.

You select the image and check that it looks OK. If not, choose another.

The ellipse can also be moved off centre, which is how you keep a ruler, a slide edge or any other non-particle object out of the analysis without cropping the image first. Drag inside the ellipse on the image to move it, shift-drag to resize, or use the Centre across and Centre down sliders for fine control. Zoom the stage to place it precisely.

Many microscopes have a brightness fade-off towards the corners. This makes it impossible to balance the "threshold" of the core image with these outer portions. Using the two Ellipses, you can choose what to include or exclude from the analysis. If your image is uniform, set them both to 150%, so there's no exclusion.

Select Threshold then slide the threshold slider to get a good division between particles and background. Chaff particles can often be excluded by having a lower value of the threshold.

It's impossible to distinguish between particles that are just touching and those that are an agglomerate, but you can use the Separate touching option to make a judgement call, using the Neck ratio to refine your choice.

Sometimes you want to exclude very small and very large particles from the analysis. Do this via D-Min & D-Max

For plotting you have a variety of choices. Number of bins is a compromise between a small value, with plenty of entries in each size "bin", but low visual resolution and a high value which gives a smoother distribution, but with fewer particles in each bin. When you have combined datasets (see below) you can go to more bins. This does not affect the calculated values. Then you can choose between Log or Frequency (as an alternative to the more usual "Probability Density)" plots and choose which of Number, Area or Volume distributions you want to see

The KDE option produces a Kernel Density Estimation plot of the histogram. It is a smart smoothing of the histogram to give a more conventional curve. KDEs are a trade-off between over- and under- smoothing. The app uses a Tuned Silverman/Scott "bandwidth" parameter, calculated from the histogram statistics.

Once you are happy with the analysis, click the Export CSV to have a record of all relevant values.

Combining data

A PSD needs many particles to be accurate, but each image has a limited number. So if you have a few different images from a wider area, you can, first, Clear Cumulative if you have a previous multi-image analysis, then for each new image, suitably thresholded, click Add to Cumulative to add the raw data to the collection. When you have enough image data, click Show Cumulative to see the PSD values based on this larger set. When you Export CSV you get the analysis of the full dataset, plus the summaries of the individual sets. If you are interested in, say, Volume D50 (the most used parameter) you can see how it varies for the individual images and how it gradually evolves to the final value. If there are large differences between samples, you may want to revise your technique. If the values are similar then you don't need to take so many images. And if you added one rogue image, you can delete it from the collection and get a revised analysis.

Calibrate scale from ruler

Drag a line along the ruler, crossing as many marks as possible. Drag either yellow end dot to reshape it, drag a mark to move it, double-click the line to add one, two-finger click a mark to remove it.

Mark colour shows how far each one sits from the fitted series: on the series, drifting, distorting the fit. Hover a mark for its number, slot and residual. The yellow end dots are the line, not marks.
